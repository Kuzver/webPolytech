Biography

Maria Skłodowska, later known as Marie Curie, was a Polish and naturalized-French physicist and chemist. She was born on 7 November 1867, in Warsaw, Congress Poland, Russian Empire. She was a pioneer in radioactivity research. She was the first woman to win a Nobel Prize, the first person and only woman to win the Nobel Prize twice, and the only person to win the Nobel Prize in two different scientific fields.

Achievements

Discovery of Radioactivity

Marie Curie, alongside her husband, pioneered the study of radioactivity, a term she coined.

Discovery of Radium and Polonium

She discovered two new chemical elements: polonium (named after her native Poland) and radium, both of which are radioactive.

Nobel Prize in Physics

She received a Nobel Prize in Physics in 1903, shared with her husband Pierre Curie and Henri Becquerel.

Nobel Prize in Chemistry

She received another Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 1911, becoming the first person to win two Nobel Prizes in two different scientific fields.

Legacy

Marie Curie's work has revolutionized science and medicine. Her discoveries paved the way for advancements in radiation therapy, nuclear medicine, and atomic energy. She remains an inspiration for women in science and for generations of researchers.
